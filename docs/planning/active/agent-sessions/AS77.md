# Role capability resolver and immutable launch manifest

## Description

**Consumed from `role-capabilities/RO02` on 2026-08-03 (RV886).** The `role-capabilities` plan was
folded into `agent-sessions` so Role has one owner and one sequence; RO02 is superseded by this
item.

Resolve an `agent-role-id` plus project/component/provider evidence into a validated, immutable
capability/launch manifest. The resolver selects and configures only the components, features,
MCP surfaces, permissions and execution capabilities a job requires, recording provenance and
explicit degraded/unsupported outcomes.

## Steps

1. Define the typed capability vocabulary (from RO01 step 2) and the role/capability models and
   loaders, behind the owning component's public API.
2. Resolve required and optional capabilities against component descriptors, feature
   declarations, MCP contributions, provider capability facts/probes and policy.
3. Produce a stable manifest and fingerprint. **Do not mutate process-global registries during
   resolution** — resolution is a pure read over evidence.
4. Enforce dependency closure, conflicts, permission boundaries and unknown-capability failures.
   Unknown means unsupported; there is no permissive default.
5. Add explain/status output showing why each capability was selected or rejected, exposing no
   secrets.
6. Add fixtures for minimal reviewer, implementer, research and unrestricted-compatibility roles
   **as tests, not as product presets** — presets are AS79's evidence-gated work.

## Validation

Carried from RO02, plus the composition constraint added on consumption:

- Resolution is **deterministic** — the same inputs produce the same manifest and fingerprint
  across processes.
- Required capabilities are complete; unrelated components and MCP surfaces are **absent** from
  the manifest, not merely unused.
- Missing evidence resolves to unknown/unsupported. No permissive fallback exists.
- Manifests are stable across processes and contain no secrets.
- The explain output justifies every selection and rejection without exposing secrets.
- **Architecture test:** the resolver is not a composition binding, and no service resolves it
  from the graph.
- **Architecture test:** the resolver imports no component or provider internals — only approved
  public read APIs.
- Resolution mutates no process-global registry; assert this directly rather than by inspection.

## Effort & Risk

Large, and the most semantically demanding item in the composition track.

RO02's stated risk carries over: this is cross-domain resolution, and the failure mode is the
resolver reaching into component and provider internals because the narrow typed facts it needs
do not exist yet. Expect real work on the *owning* components to expose those facts, and treat
that as part of this item rather than as an obstacle to route around.

Two risks added on consumption:

- **Vocabulary sprawl.** A capability vocabulary spanning components, features, MCP, permissions
  and resources is exactly the kind of thing that grows to cover hypothetical cases. §3's
  evidence-led bar applies per term: no capability term without a demonstrated Role that needs it.
- **Becoming a second capability model.** PC owns provider capabilities and AS29 owns
  harness/session-surface capabilities. This item owns neither and must consume both.

## Standards

- `arch-standards` §1 Dependency Boundaries — the resolver reads narrow typed public facts and
  imports no component or provider internals; `providers_api` is the only approved cross-component
  import.
- `arch-standards` §2 Extensions and Configuration — the capability vocabulary is
  configuration-declared with no provider-name conditionals (RO01's explicit requirement).
- `arch-standards` §3 Evidence-led scope — no capability term without a demonstrated Role
  requiring it; role fixtures are tests, not shipped presets.
- `arch-standards` §3 Ownership and Abstraction — PC owns provider capabilities, AS29 owns
  surface capabilities, AS61 owns the Role type. This item owns resolution only.
- `arch-standards` §6 Platform and Provider Boundaries — provider facts are consumed through the
  approved narrow API.
- AS59 Rule 0 — the resolver is component-internal and is never a composition binding.

## Acceptance Criteria

- One `agent-role-id` plus project/component/provider evidence resolves to a validated, immutable
  manifest with a stable fingerprint, reproducible across processes.
- The manifest contains **only** the capabilities the role requires. Unrelated components,
  features and MCP surfaces are absent from it, not merely disabled.
- Missing or unknown evidence resolves to unsupported. No permissive fallback exists anywhere in
  the resolver.
- Every capability term in the vocabulary traces to a demonstrated Role that needs it
  (`arch-standards` §3).
- The vocabulary contains no provider-name conditionals — RO01's explicit requirement, carried
  over.
- The resolver imports no component or provider internals; it reads narrow typed public facts
  only, with `providers_api` the sole approved cross-component import.
- The resolver is **not** a composition binding, and no service resolves it from the graph
  (AS59 Rule 0).
- Resolution mutates no process-global registry.
- Explain/status output justifies every selection and rejection and exposes no secrets; manifests
  contain no secrets.
- Role fixtures ship as tests, not as product presets — preset selection is AS79's evidence-gated
  decision.
- Provider capability facts come from the PC catalogue and surface capabilities from AS29. This
  item defines no second capability model for either.

## Why this is a separate item from AS61, not part of it

AS61 deliberately stops short of this. Its `Do not add` list forbids runtime tool-set
normalization, runtime tool-policy resolution and enforcement, and its Acceptance criteria say
"runtime tool policy is at most an inert placeholder **for a future owning plan**". This is that
plan. AS61 reserves the seam; this item fills it.

AS64 is also distinct and stays distinct: it is admission-time checking of Role requirements
against surface and model facts, and it states "tool/MCP/permission capability matching is out of
scope". That matching is this item's.

So the three layers are: **AS61** the Role type, **AS64** admission checks against surface/model
facts, **AS77** capability resolution into a launch manifest. Nothing may drift between them
without an explicit review.

## Owned here from RO01

RO01 step 2's typed capability vocabulary — spanning AUDiaGentic components, features, MCP
tools/servers, provider execution capabilities, permissions and resource needs, **without
provider-name conditionals** — lands here rather than in AS61. A vocabulary with no resolver is a
contract fixed against an absent consumer, which is the sequencing error AS59's Effort & Risk
section argues against.

RO01's least-capability default also lands here: absent or unknown evidence resolves to
unsupported, never to permissive.

## Composition boundary (added on consumption; not in RO02)

AS59 Stage 1 landed the composition facility and its Rule 0: **a binding names a component public
surface or a foundation capability, never an internal collaborator.**

The resolver is component-internal. It is therefore **not** a composition binding. It is
constructed behind the owning component's public surface, and only that surface may ever appear
in the graph. Do not add a `role-capability-resolver` binding, a resolver registry, or a global
lookup — AS61's `Do not add` list already forbids the equivalent for Role, and the same applies
here.

The evidence sources the resolver reads (component descriptors, feature declarations, MCP
contributions, provider capability facts) are read through **narrow typed public APIs**. RO02's
Effort & Risk section made this the central risk and it is carried over unchanged: component and
provider owners expose narrow typed facts; the resolver imports none of their internals. Only
`providers_api` is an approved cross-component import (ARCHITECTURE_STANDARDS §1's table).

## Relationship to the provider capability model

The `provider-capability-model` plan (PC) governs the provider-side capability catalogue. This
item **consumes** those facts and must not define a second capability model for providers. If the
resolver appears to need a provider capability that PC does not express, that is a PC change,
raised there — not a local vocabulary extension here.

Coordinate likewise with PT01 (Provider Telemetry Capability Model) rather than duplicating it;
RO02's Notes flagged this and it still holds.

- Provider capability catalogue (`providers/descriptors/capability_catalogue.py`, `_capabilities.yaml`) -- domains `exec`, `mcp`, `lsp`, `perm`, `model`, `surface`, `acp`, `hook`, `plugin`, `file`, `cli`, `obs`.
- Two descriptor-level tier facts consumed today at gateway admission (`agents/gateway/api.py:_resolve_provider_isolation_tier` -> `providers_api.get_provider_execution_isolation_tier`, and `gateway/queue/contention.py`):
