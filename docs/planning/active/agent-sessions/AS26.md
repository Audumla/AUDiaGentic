# Recover proven orphaned turns through task-owned cleanup

## Description

Detect a proven dead owned harness during an active turn and recover without manually releasing asyncio locks or mutating scheduler sets. Track the owning turn task/request and process evidence, move through normal lifecycle, and let existing owner finally blocks reclaim lock/semaphore/accounting exactly once.

## Steps

1. Add explicit current-turn task/request ownership and owned/external process evidence from AS17/AS08. 2. Reaper may inspect non-quiescent handles and acts only on proven death; unknown remains conservative. 3. Declare agents.session.orphaned in the agents event catalog and record one redacted timeline outcome. 4. Transition active to closing, cancel/signal the owner task or close transport, and let its finally release lock and queue slot. 5. After a bounded closing deadline, force close only through proven ownership foundation seams. 6. Terminalize failed-orphaned and coordinate request outcome with SH07; do not create another request-state authority. 7. Never signal external processes.

## Files

Session turn ownership/reaper/lifecycle projection, queue idempotent finalization if needed, event descriptor/constants, AS17 process evidence seam, focused unit/integration/Docker tests.

## Validation

Killing a fake ACP child mid-turn is detected; active→closing→failed occurs once; one orphan event/timeline; slot/lock reclaimed once by owner cleanup; pending turn fails without deadlock; unknown/external process is not killed; force-close deadline leaves no owned descendant. Docker proves real process-tree mutation and cleanup.

## Effort & Risk

Mid operational correctness. Main risk is double cleanup, addressed by task ownership and existing finally paths.

## Standards

Architecture Standards; Architecture Guidelines; Observability Standards where events/timelines are involved; docs/reference/PROVIDER_CAPABILITY_REFERENCE. Reuse foundation paths, workflow, events, process evidence, and managed-service seams before adding component-local machinery.
