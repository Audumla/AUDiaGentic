# Migrate to explicit role presets and policy gates

## Description

**Consumed from `role-capabilities/RO04` on 2026-08-03 (RV886).** RO04 is superseded by this item.

Introduce approved role presets and migration tooling **only after real job evidence** identifies
useful capability bundles. Replace generic all-or-nothing launches where safe, retain an
explicitly named unrestricted-compatibility role during a bounded transition, and add policy gates
preventing silent capability escalation.

## Steps

1. Collect current job and workflow capability usage; derive candidate presets from that evidence.
2. Review and approve a minimal preset set — reviewer, implementer, researcher, operator — only
   where the evidence justifies each one.
3. Add migration and status tooling that **reports** unresolved or excess capabilities before any
   behaviour changes.
4. Require explicit authorization for privileged capabilities, and record safe audit and timeline
   facts.
5. Migrate selected workflows incrementally, with rollback.
6. Remove the role-less compatibility default introduced by AS78 once all consumers are explicit,
   or record an approved external obligation for keeping it.

## Validation

Carried from RO04:

- Migrated workflows pass with **measurably fewer** exposed capabilities — a before/after count,
  not an assertion that they still work.
- Attempts to request undeclared or privileged capabilities **fail closed** and are auditable.
- **No hidden fallback silently restores all components.** Assert directly; this is the failure
  this item exists to prevent.

Added on consumption:

- Every approved preset traces to recorded usage evidence from step 1. A preset with no evidence
  is not shipped.
- AS78's role-less compatibility default is either removed, or its retention is recorded as an
  approved external obligation under §4.

## Effort & Risk

Medium, and deferred rather than merely later — starting before AS78 has produced real usage
evidence would defeat the item's purpose.

Risk carried from RO04: presets encoding guesses. Risk added on consumption: this item is the last
in the chain and therefore the one most likely to be skipped once roles "work", leaving the
unrestricted default permanently in place. That outcome would leave the system with role
machinery and no capability reduction — all of the cost and none of the benefit.

## Standards

- `arch-standards` §3 Evidence-led scope — presets derive from measured usage, never from
  anticipated need.
- `arch-standards` §4 Public Contracts — this item owns removal of AS78's bounded compatibility
  default; retention requires a recorded, approved external obligation.
- `arch-standards` §7 Enforcement — policy gates carry automated checks; escalation attempts fail
  closed.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Every approved preset traces to recorded usage evidence collected in step 1. A preset with no
  supporting evidence is not shipped — this is the item's defining constraint, not a preference.
- Migrated workflows run with **measurably fewer** exposed capabilities, evidenced by a
  before/after count rather than by continuing to pass.
- Requests for undeclared or privileged capabilities fail closed and are auditable.
- **No hidden fallback silently restores all components.** Asserted directly.
- The unrestricted-compatibility role is selectable by explicit name, visible in audit and
  timeline facts, and removable — never an implicit default.
- Privileged capabilities require explicit authorization, with safe audit and timeline facts
  recorded.
- AS78's role-less compatibility default is removed, or its retention is recorded as an approved
  external obligation under `arch-standards` §4.
- Migration is incremental with rollback available at each step.
- No new role semantics are introduced here — the Role type stays AS61's and capability
  resolution stays AS77's.

## This item is evidence-gated by design

RO04's own Effort & Risk section stated the reason and it is the whole point of the item:
**presets created too early encode guesses and cause churn.** Do not derive presets from what
seems reasonable; derive them from measured job and workflow capability usage.

AS77 deliberately ships its reviewer/implementer/research role fixtures as *tests*, not product
presets, precisely so that this item can decide the real preset set on evidence rather than
inherit AS77's test conveniences by default.

This is adoption and cleanup. It is not a place to add new role semantics — those belong to AS61
(the Role type) or AS77 (capability resolution).

## Owned here: retiring AS78's compatibility path

AS78 introduces a documented migration default for callers that supply no role. Under
`arch-standards` §4 a compatibility path needs a named owner for its removal, and this item is
that owner. Step 6 is not optional cleanup — it is the condition under which AS78's exception to
§4 was granted.

## The unrestricted-compatibility role

RO04 required this role to be **explicitly named** rather than implicit, and that requirement is
load-bearing: an implicit "everything" default is indistinguishable from no role system at all,
and would silently survive the migration. It must be selectable by name, visible in audit and
timeline facts, and removable.

## Policy gates

A capability escalation — a job acquiring a capability its role does not declare — fails closed
and is auditable. There is no hidden fallback that silently restores all components; RO04's
Validation made this explicit and it is the single most important assertion in the item.
