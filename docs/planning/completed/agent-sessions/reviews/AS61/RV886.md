# AS61's "sole Role owner" claim was made without the role-capabilities plan in view

## Notes

Raised 2026-08-03 while surveying the plan landscape after AS59 Stage 1 closed. Not acted on —
AS61 is not in flight, and this needs an owner decision rather than an edit.

## Findings



## Conclusion

Resolved 2026-08-03 by consuming the `role-capabilities` plan into `agent-sessions`, on the
owner's instruction.

The survey finding stands — two owners for Role — but the recommended resolution in this review
("decide which item owns the Role type, narrow the other") turned out to be the wrong shape once
both sides were read in full. They were not competing for the same scope.

**AS61 explicitly defers what RO02-RO04 do.** AS61's `Do not add` list forbids runtime tool-set
normalization and runtime tool-policy resolution/enforcement, and its acceptance criteria say
runtime tool policy is "at most an inert placeholder **for a future owning plan**". RO02 *is* that
plan. AS64 likewise states that "tool/MCP/permission capability matching is out of scope". So the
RO chain was the deferred layer both AS items pointed at, not a rival definition.

The genuine overlap was confined to RO01, which mixed Role-type design (AS61's) with capability
vocabulary (the resolver's).

cheap to fix.

The SH12 sequencing hazard recorded here is carried onto AS78 and AS77, where the work actually

## The collision

AS61 ("Add minimal reusable roles") carries an `AS59 disposition` stanza reading:

> Sole Role owner. Runtime tool/MCP/permission policy stays out -- inert placeholder only.

The `role-capabilities` plan has four active P1 items covering the same concept:

- **RO01** — "Define agent-role and capability contracts distinct from agent profiles": introduces
  agent role as execution intent plus required/optional capabilities, and defines ownership,
  configuration schema, naming, composition rules, inheritance constraints and failure semantics.
- **RO02** — config-driven role capability resolver producing an immutable launch manifest.
- **RO03** — role selection integrated into job submission and shared-gateway.
- **RO04** — migration from generic all-capabilities agents to explicit role profiles.

Both define "role" and both define "required capabilities". `arch-standards` §3 requires each
durable artifact to have one owner and one lifecycle path.

## Why the claim was made in good faith

AS59's Disposition Table states its own scope in its first line: "Authority for every active
**agent-sessions** item." It never surveyed other plans, so AS61's sole-ownership claim was
asserted against the agent-sessions set only. The claim is not wrong within its stated scope — it
is just narrower than it reads.

## They are not straightforwardly duplicates

AS61 is deliberately minimal: instructions or managed instruction references, simple required
capabilities, optional output guidance, and an explicitly inert runtime-tool-policy placeholder.
Its Boundary section forbids creating a tool/MCP/permission/instruction registry.

RO01/RO02 are the opposite in ambition: capability resolution against project/component/provider
evidence, an immutable launch manifest selecting components, features, MCP surfaces, permissions
and execution capabilities, with provenance and explicit degraded/unsupported outcomes.

So one reading is that they are complementary — AS61 the minimal Role type, RO the capability
resolution layer above it. The other reading is that RO01 subsumes AS61 entirely, since RO01
explicitly includes "ownership, configuration schema, naming, composition rules" for roles.

Either reading may be correct. What cannot stand is both items proceeding while each believes it
owns the Role contract.

## Recommended resolution (documentation-only, no code)

1. Decide which item owns the **Role type and its schema**. RO01 is the more natural owner on
   scope; AS61 is the more natural owner on sequencing, since it sits in the composition track
   between AS60 and AS62 and AS62 (Agent Definition) consumes it.
2. Whichever loses ownership of the type keeps a narrowed scope and an explicit precedence note,
   in the same form as AS59's existing `keep/amend` dispositions.
3. Record the decision in both items, not one — a one-sided note is how this recurred.
4. Consider whether AS59's Disposition Table should carry an explicit "scope: agent-sessions only"
   caveat, so the next reader does not take a `sole owner` claim as repo-wide. This is cheap and
   would have prevented the ambiguity.
